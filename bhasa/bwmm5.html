The BMW M5 Touring

The BMW M5 Touring is a high-performance luxury wagon manufactured by BMW. It combines the speed and power of a sports car with the practicality of a spacious family vehicle. The M5 Touring features an aggressive design, advanced technology, and a powerful plug-in hybrid powertrain. Its long roofline and large cargo area make it useful for everyday driving and road trips, while its M performance engineering delivers an exciting driving experience. The exterior of the BMW M5 Touring features a bold front grille, sharp LED headlights, sculpted body panels, and muscular wheel arches. Its wide stance and sporty bumpers give it an aggressive appearance, while the extended roofline creates additional cargo space. At the rear, distinctive LED taillights, a roof spoiler, and four exhaust outlets emphasize its performance-focused design. Depending on the configuration, it can also feature large alloy wheels and optional carbon-fiber styling details.

The interior of the BMW M5 Touring combines luxury, comfort, and modern technology. It features BMW's curved display, which combines the digital instrument cluster and central infotainment screen. The cabin includes sport seats, a multifunction steering wheel, ambient lighting, and high-quality materials. Depending on the options selected, it can offer premium leather upholstery, heated and ventilated seats, and a high-end sound system. Unlike a traditional sports sedan, the Touring body style also provides a spacious cargo area for luggage, groceries, and other equipment.

The BMW M5 Touring uses a 4.4-liter twin-turbocharged V8 engine combined with an electric motor in a plug-in hybrid system. In the current-generation model, the combined output reaches approximately 717 horsepower. Its M xDrive all-wheel-drive system helps deliver power to the road, allowing the vehicle to accelerate from 0 to 60 mph in around 3.5 seconds. The hybrid system also allows limited electric-only driving for shorter journeys, depending on battery charge and driving conditions.
